\noindent
The Theory of Persistence (PT) derives the full phenomenology of the Standard Model from a single formal input, the arithmetic parameter $\sper = 1/2$. This preface gives the reader's map of the program: statements of the seven structural theorems $\Tzero$--$\Tsix$, of the six bridge axioms $\BAzero$--$\BAfive$, of the fixed point $\mustar = 3 + 5 + 7 = 15$, of the prime taxonomy (active $\{3,5,7\}$, echoes $\{11,13\}$, super-echoes $\{17,19,23\}$), of the arithmetic cascade principle, and of the canonical application to the fine-structure constant $1/\alpha_{\rm EM} = 137{,}035\,999\,083$ at $0{,}004$~ppb. No proof is given here; every statement points to the M1--M5 articles where the full derivation appears. This article serves as the pedagogical entry point of the series.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation: why a sieve?}
\label{sec:motivation}

The Standard Model of particle physics involves about twenty numerical parameters without theoretical derivation: three gauge couplings, the Higgs vacuum expectation value and its self-coupling, nine charged masses, three CKM mixing angles and one phase, plus the analogous neutrino-sector parameters~\cite{PDG2024}. These numbers are measured, not predicted.

The Theory of Persistence proposes that they all follow from a single arithmetic structure: the Eratosthenes sieve read as a dynamical system on the sequence $\{g_n\}_{n \geq 1}$ of consecutive prime gaps. The unique formal input of the theory is a rational number,
\[
\sper \;=\; \dfrac{1}{2},
\]
called the \emph{persistence parameter}. It quantifies the symmetric fraction of information preserved by a system under successive constraints; its exact value $1/2$ coincides with the spectral gap of the primorial transfer matrix (theorem $\Ttwo$).

From this single number, PT derives seven structural theorems $\Tzero$--$\Tsix$, six proved bridge axioms $\BAzero$--$\BAfive$, an arithmetic fixed point $\mustar = 15$, and the zero-parameter derivation of every accessible constant of the Standard Model. The program is laid out in five companion articles:
\begin{description}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
\item[M1~\cite{companion_M1}] the sieve as a dynamical field: $\Tone, \Tthree, \Tsix$, lemma $\Lzero$, Gap Fundamental Theorem.
\item[M2~\cite{companion_M2}] information geometry: Fisher metric, holonomy $\BAthree$, activation criterion $\BAfour$.
\item[M3~\cite{companion_M3}] convergence and fixed point: $\Ttwo, \Tfour, \Tfive$, closing $\BAzero \to \Tzero$.
\item[M4~\cite{companion_M4}] extended structures: sieve algebra, complex mechanics, predictive mathematics.
\item[M5~\cite{companion_M5}] bridge to physics: $\BAfive$, Pontryagin identification, reconstruction lemmas E/F/G, $\alpha_{\rm EM} = 1/137{,}036$.
\end{description}
This preface gives the map. Two extension articles complete the program: \cite{PT_GeoSpectral} for the geometric-spectral readings (persistence curve, Ricci soliton), and \cite{PT_RH} for the spectral-arithmetic RH-PT conjecture.

\subsection{Minimal formal framework}
\label{sec:crible:cadre}

Let $L^{0}$ be the category of finite pointed sets over ZFC, equipped with cardinal additivity. The \emph{maximum entropy lemma} $\Lzero$ (\cite{companion_M1}) asserts that the unique distribution on $\mathbb N$ under a finite-mean constraint is the geometric distribution, parameterised by a real $q \in (0, 1)$. This distribution is precisely the asymptotic measure of the gaps $\{g_n\}$ between consecutive primes on the 6-rough sieve.

At the arithmetic fixed point $\mustar$ (introduced in \ref{sec:crible:mustar}), the same geometric law admits \emph{two equivalent canonical parametrisations}, related by the vertex--edge involution of the sieve graph:
\begin{equation}
\qplus \;=\; 1 - \dfrac{2}{\mustar} \;=\; \dfrac{13}{15}
\qquad\text{(vertex branch, max-entropy)},
\label{eq:qplus_en}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\qminus \;=\; e^{-1/\mustar} \;=\; e^{-1/15}
\qquad\text{(edge branch, Gibbs)}.
\label{eq:qminus_en}
\end{equation}
The letter $q$ is here the standard parameter of the geometric distribution in elementary statistics; the subscripts $\pm$ distinguish the two parametrisations.

\section{Canonical application: the fine-structure constant}
\label{sec:alphaem}

The complete chain, with no adjustable parameter, leads from $\sper = 1/2$ to the numerical value of $\alpha_{\rm EM}$:
\[
\sper = 1/2 \;\xrightarrow{\Tone\text{--}\Tsix}\; \mustar = 15
\;\xrightarrow{\BAthree}\; \sinp{p}(\qplus)
\;\xrightarrow{\BAfive}\; \alpha_{\rm bare} \;=\; \prod_{p\in\{3,5,7\}}\sinp{p}(\qplus).
\]
The numerical values (computed in M5~\cite{companion_M5}) are
\[
\sinp{3}(\qplus) = 0{,}2192, \qquad
\sinp{5}(\qplus) = 0{,}1940, \qquad
\sinp{7}(\qplus) = 0{,}1726,
\]
giving the \emph{bare coupling}
\[
\alpha_{\rm bare} \;=\; 0{,}2192 \times 0{,}1940 \times 0{,}1726
\;=\; \dfrac{1}{136{,}28}.
\]
The correction by the parity channel ($p = 2$) --- binary leakage, spiral retroaction, screening by echoes $\{11, 13\}$, two-loop vacuum polarisation --- closes the gap to the measured value. The final result, with no adjustable parameter~\cite{companion_M5}:
\[
\boxed{\;\dfrac{1}{\alpha_{\rm EM}^{\rm PT}} \;=\; 137{,}035\,999\,083 \quad\text{(deviation: $0{,}004$~ppb).}\;}
\]
The measured value from the quantum Hall effect and electron anomalous magnetic moment~\cite{PDG2024} is
\[
\dfrac{1}{\alpha_{\rm EM}^{\rm exp}} \;=\; 137{,}035\,999\,084(21),
\]
agreement to experimental precision ($0{,}15$~ppb).
